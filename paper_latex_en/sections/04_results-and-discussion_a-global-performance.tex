Performance evaluation of RF across seven feature configurations on the DemogPairs test set is presented in Table~\ref{tab:VII}. Empirical experimental results indicate that the dual-domain Emotion $\oplus$ Face scheme achieves the highest performance with an Accuracy of 86.85\% and an F1-Score of 86.82\%. Conversely, the tri-domain aggregation Face $\oplus$ Emotion $\oplus$ Age exhibits a slight performance degradation to an Accuracy of 86.20\% and an F1-Score of 86.13\%. This decline is interpreted as potential partitioning challenges in the 2,304-dimensional feature space via random tree splits \cite{ref48}. All GridSearchCV configurations consistently select PCA 0.75 dimensionality reduction to balance data variance. In the single-domain category, the Face feature records the best performance with an Accuracy of 85.46\%, surpassing the Emotion domain feature at 80.60\% and the Age domain at 73.66\%.

The performance evaluation of probabilistic GNB classification across seven feature configuration variations is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:VIII}. Although this model assumes conditional independence between features, GNB exhibits a consistent performance improvement trend from the single-domain to the multi-domain category. In the single-domain category, the Age configuration yields the lowest performance with an Accuracy of 69.63\% and an F1-Score of 69.52\%, whereas the Face feature achieves an Accuracy of 82.69\%. Representation integration in the dual-domain category progressively improves accuracy, led by the Emotion $\oplus$ Face combination at 84.86\%. Peak GNB classification performance is achieved by the tri-domain configuration Face $\oplus$ Emotion $\oplus$ Age with an Accuracy of 85.05\% and an F1-Score of 85.05\%. All GNB feature configurations uniformly select PCA 0.75 dimensionality reduction based on GridSearchCV tuning results.

The performance benchmark results of multinomial LR across all feature configurations are presented in Table~\ref{tab:IX}. A key characteristic of the LR model is the retention of full feature representations, where all schemes (seven out of seven configurations) select no PCA reduction (pca=None) \cite{ref49}. Empirical evaluation demonstrates that the tri-domain configuration Face $\oplus$ Emotion $\oplus$ Age achieves the highest performance with an Accuracy of 92.73\% and an F1-Score of 92.73\% using penalty parameter $C=0.1$ and the newton-cg solver. This tri-domain achievement surpasses the best dual-domain configuration Emotion $\oplus$ Face, which achieves an Accuracy of 92.41\% and an F1-Score of 92.40\%, as well as the best single-domain configuration Face with an Accuracy of 90.60\%. These findings indicate the effectiveness of $L_2$ regularization in handling high-dimensional feature spaces without principal component compression \cite{ref50}.

The performance progression of SVM across all feature ablation stages is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:X}. Similar to the linear model, all SVM configurations consistently maintain the full representation space without PCA reduction (pca=None). Empirical evaluation demonstrates a tiered performance increase, starting from the best single-domain configuration Face with an Accuracy of 90.83\% and an F1-Score of 90.83\%. Representation integration in the dual-domain scheme Emotion $\oplus$ Face improves performance to an Accuracy of 93.29\% and an F1-Score of 93.29\%, before ultimately reaching the highest point in the tri-domain configuration Face $\oplus$ Emotion $\oplus$ Age with an Accuracy of 93.70\% and an F1-Score of 93.69\%. Regarding hyperparameters, the degree parameter with a value of 2 is active only for the poly kernel function, whereas RBF kernel-based models do not activate this parameter \cite{ref51}.

A synthesis of the 28 experiments across Table~\ref{tab:VII}, Table~\ref{tab:VIII}, Table~\ref{tab:IX}, and Table~\ref{tab:X} establishes the tri-domain SVM Face $\oplus$ Emotion $\oplus$ Age as the best-performing model with an Accuracy of 93.70\% and an F1-Score of 93.69\%. The average accuracy across all configurations highlights the superiority of SVM (91.47\%), followed by LR (90.40\%), RF (82.81\%), and GNB (79.37\%). The paired McNemar test results between Tri-Domain vs. Dual-Domain for SVM record a difference of +9 images (+0.41\%, $\chi^2 = 1.0492$, $p = 0.3057$, $p > 0.05$), whereas for RF a difference of -14 images is observed (-0.65\%, $\chi^2 = 1.4825$, $p = 0.2232$, $p > 0.05$). The absence of statistical significance indicates the phenomenon of asymptotic feature saturation, wherein the Dual-Domain Emotion $\oplus$ Face fusion has already captured the majority of the primary discriminative variance.
