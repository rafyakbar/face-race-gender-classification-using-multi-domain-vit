\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study evaluates a Tri-Domain ViT representation fusion framework (Face $\oplus$ Emotion $\oplus$ Age: 2,304 dimensions) for intersectional race and gender classification on the DemogPairs dataset. Experimental results demonstrate that tri-domain fusion serves as the best configuration across 3 of the 4 evaluated classifiers, encompassing SVM, LR, and GNB. Within the evaluated search space, the optimized SVM model with hyperparameter $C=10$ and a second-degree polynomial kernel yields the highest classification performance, namely an Accuracy of 93.70\% and an F1-Score of 93.69\%. Nevertheless, the benefit of multi-domain representation integration proves to be classifier-dependent, as RF achieves its peak performance on the dual-domain Emotion $\oplus$ Face scheme with an accuracy of 86.85\%. In addition, subgroup performance evaluation confirms that the F1-Score values across all six demographic subgroups are stably distributed within the range of 91.74\% to 96.14\%.

Despite these achievements, this study has several limitations that open avenues for future research. First, the applied dataset partitioning scheme represents a deliberate limitation, wherein the in-distribution benchmark protocol focuses on evaluating isolated representations at the image level. Second, the demographic scope of this investigation is limited to three macro-racial groups (Asian, Black, and White) in the DemogPairs dataset; thus, the empirical findings do not yet comprehensively cover multiracial populations or other ethnic groups. Third, feature representation extraction is conducted separately (offline) utilizing frozen pre-trained ViT backbones without simultaneous end-to-end fine-tuning. Fourth, the experimental evaluation remains focused on controlled laboratory environment images, such that model robustness against extreme pose variations, occlusions, and in-the-wild illumination requires further investigation. Based on these limitations, future research directions are recommended to formulate cross-identity generalization testing through strictly subject-disjoint partitioning that ensures the same subjects do not appear simultaneously in both training and test sets. Furthermore, future work should explore cross-modal adaptive attention fusion mechanisms (cross-attention transformer fusion) trained end-to-end, extend evaluation to large-scale heterogeneous datasets such as FairFace or UTKFace, and integrate visual explainability methods such as Attention Rollout or Grad-CAM to uphold artificial intelligence accountability principles with human-in-the-loop oversight.
