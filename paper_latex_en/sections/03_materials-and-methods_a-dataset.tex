The dataset used in this study is DemogPairs \cite{ref25}, a dataset specifically designed for facial recognition across demographic groups. This dataset contains a total of 10,800 facial images distributed evenly across six intersectional classes. The six subgroups encompass cross-combinations of three macro-race groups and two gender groups, namely Asian Females, Asian Males, Black Females, Black Males, White Females, and White Males, with a uniform allocation of exactly 1,800 images per class. This balanced data structure provides a controlled evaluation condition to objectively compare model performance across subgroups, while simultaneously mitigating distortions arising from sample size disparities \cite{ref20}. The visual characteristics of the six demographic subgroups are illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:2}.

To ensure the integrity of empirical testing, the dataset is partitioned using an 80/20 stratified split protocol with random\_state=42 and parameter stratify=y. This partition yields 8,640 training images with 1,440 samples per class and 2,160 independent held-out test images with 360 samples per class across the six intersectional classes, as detailed in Table~\ref{tab:I}. This partitioning completely isolates the held-out test set, ensuring that all test data remain strictly unseen during hyperparameter tuning as well as PCA and Scaler fitting. In the image standardization stage, each facial image is converted into a 3-channel Red, Green, Blue (RGB) format, resized to a resolution of $224 \times 224$ pixels, and its pixel intensity is scaled from the range [0, 255] to [0, 1] for the transformer models.
