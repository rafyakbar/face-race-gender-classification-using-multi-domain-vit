\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Automated recognition of facial demographic attributes plays a pivotal role in various intelligent application domains, including digital forensic systems, biometric access control, human-computer interaction, and personalized interactive services. However, numerous studies have reported that facial recognition systems frequently exhibit performance disparities across particular demographic subgroups, with substantial accuracy degradation consistently observed in female groups and darker-skinned populations. These accuracy disparities stem from diverse fundamental factors, encompassing imbalanced training data distributions across public datasets and the limitations of conventional feature extractors in capturing visual representations invariant to pose variations and natural visual degradations. The limitations of conventional systems in coping with these visual variations emphasize that fair and accurate demographic recognition still confronts significant obstacles, thereby necessitating novel approaches capable of comprehensively modeling demographic attributes.

The majority of facial attribute recognition literature formulates race and gender classification as two separate tasks solved independently. This isolated approach disregards the inherent nature of facial visual identity and fails to explicitly evaluate recognition performance at the intersections of demographic groups, such as Black females or Asian females. Consequently, performance degradations concentrated within specific intersectional subgroups escape the detection of global metrics, thereby limiting a comprehensive understanding of model reliability in real-world deployment. A unified intersectional classification formulation under a balanced evaluation setting provides an objective comparative benchmark to fairly measure performance variations across subgroups. However, intersectional classification modeling demands rich, high-capacity visual representations to distinguish decision boundaries between subgroups characterized by a high degree of visual overlap, thereby requiring a visual representation extraction architecture that is robust against phenotypic variations and facial expressions.

The development of facial attribute recognition methodologies has substantially transformed from handcrafted feature extraction approaches toward deep learning and ViT architectures. In the era of Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures, numerous studies explored demographic feature extraction by utilizing specific facial regions such as the central face area \cite{ref1}, multi-scale multi-task frameworks for simultaneous age and gender estimation \cite{ref2}, and deep CNN models for ethnicity recognition \cite{ref3}. Recent developments demonstrate the superiority of ViTs in modeling global spatial dependencies across image patches via the Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) mechanism, opening avenues for in-depth analysis of visual representation bias \cite{ref4}, while the utilization of synthetic generative representations has also been developed to mitigate demographic disparities \cite{ref5}. In addition, the integration of parallel attention mechanisms \cite{ref6}, the application of ViTs for facial gender classification \cite{ref7}, and the exploration of multi-axis transformer architectures \cite{ref8} have further enriched the diversity of visual representation modeling. Furthermore, explorations into combining cross-domain ViT representations have begun through the integration of dual facial and emotional features to enhance the discrimination of race and gender attributes \cite{ref9}, \cite{ref10}. Nonetheless, the exploration of comprehensive multi-domain representation fusion that simultaneously integrates structural biometric cues, emotional expression dynamics, and facial biological aging morphology still requires an integrated empirical investigation.

Although the exploration of ViTs and vision models has progressed rapidly, three major research gaps remain insufficiently resolved. First, the majority of prior studies rely on single-domain facial biometric representations without simultaneously integrating dynamic affective cues, causing the developed models to struggle in distinguishing subgroups with a high degree of phenotypic overlap. Second, demographic recognition frequently exhibits high sensitivity to facial morphological changes associated with advancing age because the employed feature extraction does not incorporate biological aging representations. Third, comparative evaluations regarding decision boundary behaviors among downstream classifier models encompassing linear, probabilistic, ensemble, and kernel-based models, along with their interactions with feature scaling and dimensionality reduction within the transformer representation space, remain substantially limited. To bridge the limitations of single-domain representations and evaluate these downstream classifier decision boundaries, this study proposes an integration of three ViT representation domains combined with a structured classical classifier pipeline optimization.

To address these research gaps, this study proposes a Tri-Domain ViT feature fusion framework that combines three task-specific latent representations for intersectional demographic classification from facial images. The proposed framework integrates three complementary representation domains, encompassing facial biometric geometric representations, dynamic affective expression cues, and biological aging morphological characteristics from task-specific pre-trained ViT backbone models into a unified representation via a feature concatenation operation. Latent feature extraction is conducted offline from frozen pre-trained backbone models, thereby preserving computational efficiency while isolating visual representations from the variability of retraining procedures. The resulting feature vectors are subsequently evaluated systematically through a series of single-domain, dual-domain, and tri-domain ablation schemes across four groups of classical machine learning algorithms: RF, GNB, LR, and SVM. All downstream classification architectures are optimized through a Stratified Cross-Validation procedure combined with an isolated modular pipeline of feature scaling and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) dimensionality reduction, ensuring the prevention of data leakage and the establishment of robust decision boundaries across demographic subgroups.

The main scientific contributions of this study are summarized into four primary points as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A Tri-Domain ViT feature fusion framework integrating face-associated biometric representations, expression-related representations, and age-associated facial representations into a unified latent feature vector for intersectional demographic classification.
\item An empirical comparative benchmark across multiple feature ablation schemes and classical machine learning classifiers optimized via Stratified Cross-Validation hyperparameter tuning, examining decision boundary behavior across linear, probabilistic, ensemble, and kernel-based models.
\item Competitive classification performance on the DemogPairs dataset, achieving higher reported performance compared to the evaluated single-domain and dual-domain configurations on the majority of classifiers.
\item A granular intersectional subgroup performance analysis, evaluating subgroup-level classification performance across six demographic subgroups, providing an in-depth characterization of model behaviors across racial and gender intersections while systematically revealing the consistency of multi-domain feature representations on independent evaluation cohorts without exhibiting acute performance degradation.
\end{enumerate}
