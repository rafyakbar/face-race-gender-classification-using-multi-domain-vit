\section{Related Works}
\label{sec:related_works}

The evolution of facial demographic attribute recognition methodologies has transformed from conventional facial part-based descriptors and handcrafted feature extraction toward deep representation architectures. In the early phase, local region-based approaches exploited middle-face geometry to recognize ethnicity and minimize the interference of background variations \cite{ref1}. However, classical CNN-based extractors often exhibit sharp accuracy degradation when confronted with extreme illumination variations as well as cross-racial recognition disparities or the Other-Race Effect phenomenon. Increasing the depth of convolutional networks has been proven to enhance visual feature discrimination across diverse populations \cite{ref3}. In addition, the integration of parallel feature-sharing mechanisms with visual attention was designed to strengthen correlations between facial attributes while simultaneously reducing local representation bias \cite{ref6}.

The application of the ViT architecture in the facial biometrics domain provides visual modeling capabilities that are fundamentally different from conventional approaches. Through the MHSA mechanism, transformer models are capable of capturing global spatial dependencies across facial image patches directly without being constrained by rigid local receptive fields \cite{ref4}. Such structural characteristics have proven effective in mapping demographic attributes such as gender on standardized-resolution facial images \cite{ref7}. Alongside standard transformer models, the exploration of hybrid architectures such as MaxViT combines window-based attention and grid attention to process spatial representations hierarchically \cite{ref8}. Although transformer architectures offer high global representation flexibility, the empirical effectiveness of demographic class separation heavily depends on the quality and characteristics of the training data used.

Investigations into performance disparities in computer vision systems have driven the development of evaluation protocols based on balanced class distributions. Testing on balanced datasets provides controlled experimental conditions to objectively measure accuracy differences across demographic subgroups without distortions from sample size imbalances, although a balanced distribution does not necessarily eliminate social biases inherent in the data. Latent representation analyses demonstrate that transformer models remain susceptible to reproducing social biases originating from large-scale pre-training corpora \cite{ref4}. To mitigate performance disparities across racial and gender groups, generative augmentation techniques based on StyleGAN2 have been utilized to synthesize viewpoint variations that narrow classification error gaps \cite{ref5}. Such approaches underscore the importance of comprehensive evaluation across demographic subgroups.

The limitations of single-domain representations in distinguishing facial visual variations have motivated the exploration of multi-scale and multi-task fusion architectures. Multi-task learning approaches have proven capable of extracting dependencies between age and gender estimation through the simultaneous processing of multiple facial image patches \cite{ref2}. Parallel representation-sharing mechanisms have also been developed to combine various affective attributes simultaneously \cite{ref6}, while the utilization of generative viewpoints enriches feature variations across demographic groups \cite{ref5}. Furthermore, the integration of ViT-based dual latent features combining facial biometrics and emotional expressions is capable of enhancing intersectional classification accuracy \cite{ref9}. Combining biometric features with age representations also demonstrates complementary informational contributions \cite{ref10}, thereby substantiating the empirical potential of fusing cross-domain latent representations.

The selection of downstream classifier models exerts a substantial influence on the effectiveness of decision boundary separation in high-dimensional feature spaces. End-to-end SoftMax classification layers are commonly applied in convolutional neural network architectures to map class probabilities directly \cite{ref1}, \cite{ref8}. On the other hand, kernel-based algorithms such as SVM and adaptive kernel models exhibit high robustness in separating complex non-linear decision boundaries without requiring exhaustive weight updating processes \cite{ref3}. In addition, tree-based ensemble models such as RF have proven effective in modeling hierarchical interactions among demographic data through multi-level partitioning \cite{ref2}, whereas probabilistic approaches such as GNB offer high inference efficiency. Such class separation behaviors confirm that each classification model possesses unique decision boundary characteristics.

Based on the reviewed literature, this study occupies a strategic position in comprehensively bridging the gaps in facial demographic attribute modeling. In contrast to prior studies that rely on single-domain representations or partial dual-domain fusions, this study proposes a tri-domain fusion framework that integrates facial biometric geometric representations, dynamic expression affective cues, and biological aging morphological characteristics based on frozen pre-trained ViT models in a unified manner. These multi-domain latent representations are subsequently integrated with an optimized SVM pipeline to form robust non-linear decision boundaries. A comprehensive comparative evaluation is conducted on the balanced DemogPairs dataset to analyze intersectional race and gender classification performance along with its disparity variations.
