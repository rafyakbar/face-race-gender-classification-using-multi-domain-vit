A direct empirical comparison between the proposed framework and prior studies on the DemogPairs dataset is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:XII}. The proposed Tri-Domain ViT + SVM model (Face $\oplus$ Emotion $\oplus$ Age) achieves an Accuracy of 93.70\%, Precision of 93.72\%, Recall of 93.70\%, and F1-Score of 93.69\%. These quantitative achievements surpass the performance reported for the Dual-ViT + SVM model with 92.41\% Accuracy and 92.38\% F1-Score \cite{ref19}, as well as the MD-ViT + XGBoost model with 89.07\% Accuracy and 89.01\% F1-Score \cite{ref20}. This comparison positions the scientific contribution of this paper as a unified systematic tri-domain evaluation featuring significance testing and decision boundary analyses that extend beyond prior conference proceedings studies \cite{ref19}. All comparative figures are cited directly from their respective publications as a contextual positioning on the same reference dataset.

The seminal research introducing the DemogPairs dataset by Hupont and Fern{\'a}ndez \cite{ref25} was exclusively designed as an identity verification benchmark (identity verification ROC across 58.3 million image pairs) and did not perform 6-class classification training (closed-set intersectional classification). Therefore, the present study, together with related prior work \cite{ref19}, \cite{ref20}, pioneers and provides the first comprehensive internally controlled baselines for 6-class intersectional demographic classification on the DemogPairs dataset. In addition to evaluating global aggregate metrics, this investigation conducts an in-depth subgroup disparity analysis on the best tri-domain configuration to transparently map performance variations across demographic subgroups. This thorough evaluation demonstrates classification stability across intersectional race and gender categories, whereas subgroup performance variation information was not reported in prior comparative literature \cite{ref19}, \cite{ref20}.
