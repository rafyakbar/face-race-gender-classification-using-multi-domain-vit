The granular classification performance profile of the best-performing tri-domain SVM model (Face $\oplus$ Emotion $\oplus$ Age) across the six demographic subgroups is thoroughly evaluated via OvR metrics in Table~\ref{tab:XI}. All demographic subgroups successfully achieve an F1-Score exceeding 91.00\%, ranging from 91.74\% for Black Females to 96.14\% for White Males, resulting in a 4.40\% disparity gap. Concurrently, OvR Accuracy values fall within the range of 97.31\% to 98.70\% with a 1.39 pp disparity. Methodologically, high OvR Accuracy values are influenced by the 5:1 negative sample dominance in binary OvR evaluation, such that this metric is not positioned as the sole indicator of performance disparity across subgroups.

A comparison of subgroup performance profiles between the SVM model and the linear LR classifier under the tri-domain configuration is presented in Table~\ref{tab:XI}. The tri-domain LR model likewise demonstrates high performance consistency, with F1-Score achievements exceeding 91.00\% across all demographic classes, spanning from 91.36\% for Asian Females to 95.58\% for White Males with a range disparity difference of 4.22\%. The White Males subgroup consistently records the highest classification performance across both classifiers. These empirical findings demonstrate that the tri-domain representation fusion yields robust visual representations that are stably distributed across all demographic subgroups, in both linear and non-linear models. Nevertheless, this subgroup evaluation reflects granular per-class performance rather than a comprehensive algorithmic fairness audit.
