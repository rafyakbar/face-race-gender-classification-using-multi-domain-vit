\subsection{Comparison with Prior Studies}
\label{sec:comparison_with_prior_studies}
\label{sec:comparison_prior}

The empirical comparison of the proposed framework against prior studies and internal baselines on the DemogPairs dataset is presented in Table~\ref{tab:16}. The evaluation is structured into two data partitioning protocol groups, namely the Stratified protocol group and the Subject-Disjoint protocol group. In the Stratified protocol group representing a contextual comparison, Proposed Tri-Domain + SVM achieves the highest performance with an Accuracy of 93.70\%, Precision of 93.72\%, Recall of 93.70\%, and F1-Score of 93.69\%. This achievement surpasses the prior models Dual-ViT + SVM with an Accuracy of 92.41\%~\cite{ref19} and MD-ViT + XGBoost with an Accuracy of 89.07\%~\cite{ref20}. In the Subject-Disjoint protocol group evaluating generalization without identity leakage, Proposed Tri-Domain + LR leads the benchmark with an Accuracy of 88.66\%, followed by Proposed Tri-Domain + SVM (88.61\%). Tri-domain fusion consistently outperforms single-domain and dual-domain configurations.

This research positioning affirms a crucial methodological qualification between the two evaluation protocols. Results under the Stratified protocol are classified purely as a contextual comparison because random image-level partitioning allows subject identities to overlap between the training and test sets, compounded by potential preprocessing variations across studies. The performance difference between the Stratified protocol evaluation (93.70\%) and the primary Subject-Disjoint benchmark (88.66\%) indicates pseudo-optimism resulting from identity data leakage of 5.04~pp (a difference of -5.04~pp, as well as differences of -4.07~pp for Proposed Tri-Domain + LR and -5.09~pp for Proposed Tri-Domain + SVM). Implementing the strict Subject-Disjoint protocol across 480 training subjects and 120 held-out test subjects on the DemogPairs corpus~\cite{ref25}, which was originally designed for facial identity pair verification, guarantees the absolute elimination of identity leakage, thereby yielding a reliable 6-class intersectional classification evaluation in real-world scenarios.
